    Research notes

    What makes a pulsed cue informative but tolerable

    • Use rhythm for "how far off", not pitch. Pulse rate is a more robust urgency cue than frequency (Gonzalez, Lewis & Baldwin 2012). Parking sensors step from slow to fast beeps as distance shrinks (Mazda manual). Variometers pulse faster with climb and stay silent in a dead band (EP0024904).
    • Short, decaying pulses cost less. Loudness integrates over about 200 ms, so a 10 ms tick sounds far quieter than a tone at the same peak (SFU handbook, ASA 1993). At 20 % duty, average power is 7 dB below a continuous tone.
    • Sounds like everyday sounds irritate less than flat beeps. Edworthy's review reports auditory icons as less tiring and irritating than tonal alarms (Forum Acusticum 2025, 2025 study). IEC 60601-1-8 Amd 2 moved toward them. Very steep, loud onsets startle.
    • Modulation rate sets the character. Fluctuation strength peaks near 4 Hz (siren-like) and roughness near 70 Hz (rasp) (Siemens). The old pitch-tracking sine wobbled at foot-correction speed, about 1–4 Hz. Pulses at 3–12 /s read as rhythm and 10–25 Hz as a purr.
    • Bass goes through walls. Wall loss rises about 6 dB per octave under the mass law (simulator), and quiet bass must be played louder to be heard (equal-loudness). Keep fundamentals at 400–1200 Hz and energy below about 2.5 kHz, since sharpness rises above 3 kHz.
    • Silence in band is good training design. Bandwidth feedback, given only outside a tolerance, improves consistency (Sherwood 1988). Constant concurrent feedback can breed dependence (Sigrist et al. 2013), so plan a fade-out or blind mode.
    • Direction is optional. People identify only about 5–6 absolute pitch levels (Pollack via Miller 1956), so a continuous pitch map is hard to read. Two fixed pitches (F) or a rising or falling pair (G) can be learned.

    Recommended shortlist

    1. A · Rolled "tr". It is the "tr-tr-tr" you asked for. Noise has no pitch, so it cannot turn into a beeper. The 250–1000 Hz band keeps it soft and leaks less than bass, and size is shown by roll length rather than pitch.
    2. B · Wood tok, with "Faster when further off" on. A natural, auditory-icon-like knock at about 10 % duty. It is likely the easiest to live with for an hour, and the switch adds parking-sensor size information.
    3. F · Variometer, if you want to hear over versus under (Trace drills, or a later blind mode). Rate shows size and two fixed pitches a fourth apart show direction, with no glides.
